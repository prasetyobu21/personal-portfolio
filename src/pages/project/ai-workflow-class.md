## A new way of working

At bitbybit, the way our product team works has changed how I think about the design process.

Our product team can translate UI designs directly into frontend implementation. That makes the workflow feel more effective: there is less friction between a design and the interface people eventually use, and fewer differences introduced along the way.

That experience made me curious about how far I could take my own workflow. I started exploring a second brain, building an MCP for my personal AI brain, and finding ways to carry context through my work. The more I experimented, the more useful AI became as part of a connected process.

Then I started wondering: **how many product designers are still working without this kind of AI workflow?**

## A conversation

I caught up with a former intern and asked about how he was working. He told me he wasn't using an AI workflow yet and was still following a more traditional process.

That conversation stayed with me. If someone I knew was in that position, perhaps many other designers were too. Some might not have had the chance to try AI at work. Others might have wanted to explore it but didn't know where to begin.

I began thinking about a small, free class: a place where product and UI/UX designers could learn how to enter an AI workflow and see how the pieces fit together.

## Asking the community

Before putting the class together, I shared the idea [on Threads](https://www.threads.com/share/EBFQWZRqR/). I asked whether designers who hadn't yet started using AI in their workflow would be interested in a free mini class.

I described what I hoped to cover: using AI across the workflow, and making concepts like AI harnesses, a second brain, and Git easier to approach.

## From idea to class

I created the **AI Workflow Class** as a practical online class for designers. The goal was to help people see how AI could support their work from an early idea through design and implementation, while keeping their own judgment involved throughout.

## Inside the class

The first session was about moving beyond a chat window. I wanted to show how an AI assistant can use the context, instructions, and tools around it to help carry a task forward—and why the person using it still needs to review the result.

We broke down ideas that can feel technical at first: models, agents, harnesses, MCP connections, and reusable skills. Then we explored the second brain: a collection of notes, decisions, and references that can give an assistant useful context when it has the right access. I shared my own setup as one example, while encouraging participants to choose what works for them.

The goal was to make the workflow tangible: start with a task, bring in relevant context, use the right tools, and review what comes back. A designer can use that loop to move from a brief toward a design and eventually implementation, while keeping responsibility for the decisions along the way.

## Participant feedback

Hearing what participants took away from the class was one of the most rewarding parts of the experience. They shared how the explanations, practical examples, and discussion of the second brain helped them picture an AI workflow for their own work.
